Social Security Retirement Benefits in 2026

Clear explanation of eligibility, Full Retirement Age, the best time to claim (62 vs FRA vs 70), the 2.8% COLA, earnings test rules, and real examples.

Who Can Get Retirement Benefits?

You generally qualify if you have earned at least 40 work credits (about 10 years of work) and are age 62 or older.

  • U.S. citizens and qualified non-citizens with a valid Social Security number
  • In 2026 you earn 1 credit for every $1,890 in covered earnings (maximum 4 credits per year)

Your Full Retirement Age (FRA)

FRA is the age at which you receive 100% of your benefit. Claiming earlier permanently reduces it. Delaying past FRA increases it.

When Should You Claim? 62 vs FRA vs 70

Your claiming age has a large effect on your monthly payment for the rest of your life.

Claiming Age Effect on Benefit Example (FRA benefit $2,071)
Age 62 About 30% reduction ≈ $1,450 / month
Full Retirement Age 100% of benefit $2,071 / month
Age 70 +24% from delayed credits ≈ $2,568 / month

Delayed retirement credits are worth 8% per year between FRA and age 70.

Working While Receiving Benefits (Earnings Test)

If you claim before Full Retirement Age and continue working, SSA may temporarily withhold some benefits.

  • Under FRA for the full year: $24,480 limit → $1 withheld for every $2 over the limit
  • Year you reach FRA: $65,160 limit → $1 withheld for every $3 over the limit
  • After FRA: No earnings limit

Withheld benefits are not lost forever — SSA recalculates your benefit at Full Retirement Age.

Real-World Claiming Examples

Claims at 62

Needs income now. Accepts a permanent reduction of about 30% to start benefits early.

Waits until FRA

Receives 100% of the calculated benefit. A balanced choice for many people.

Delays to age 70

Earns the maximum delayed credits (+24%). Best for those with longer life expectancy and other income sources.

Still Working Under FRA

Must watch the $24,480 earnings limit or face temporary benefit reductions.

Divorced Spouse

May claim on an ex-spouse’s record if the marriage lasted 10+ years and other rules are met.

Maximum Earner at FRA

In 2026 the highest possible benefit at Full Retirement Age is $4,152 per month.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I claim Social Security?

It depends on your health, other income, and life expectancy. Delaying to 70 gives the highest monthly benefit if you can afford to wait.

Can I suspend benefits after I start them?

Yes, but only after you reach Full Retirement Age. You can voluntarily suspend and restart later for a higher amount.

Will working reduce my benefits?

Only if you are under Full Retirement Age and earn more than the annual limit. After FRA there is no reduction for working.

How are benefits taxed?

Up to 85% of your benefits may be taxable depending on your combined income. See our Tax Guide for details and a calculator.
